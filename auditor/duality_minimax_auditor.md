# Duality and Minimax Auditor

## 1. Identify the exact operation

Is the derivation using:

- Lagrangian duality;
- Fenchel conjugacy;
- conic duality;
- generalized moment duality;
- Sion-type minimax;
- lopsided minimax;
- interchange of expectation and optimization;
- interchange of limit and optimization?

Do not group these under “strong duality.”

## 2. Primal and dual spaces

State whether variables live in finite-dimensional Euclidean space, a function space, or a measure space.

A finite-dimensional Slater theorem cannot be transplanted to an infinite-dimensional measure problem without a valid theorem.

## 3. Weak duality first

Derive the weak-duality inequality and check the sign convention. This catches many max/min mistakes.

## 4. Strong-duality condition map

For the chosen theorem, verify:

- convexity/concavity;
- properness;
- closure/semicontinuity;
- feasibility;
- relative interior/Slater-type condition;
- compactness/coercivity if required;
- topology on probability measures;
- integrability/growth conditions.

## 5. Minimax

For every `inf sup = sup inf`, write the theorem and map:

- convex variable/set;
- concave variable/set;
- compactness or alternative lopsided condition;
- semicontinuity.

If only `sup inf <= inf sup` is known, do not swap.

## 6. Attainment versus zero gap

Zero duality gap does not imply both problems attain optimizers. Separate:

- value equality;
- primal attainment;
- dual attainment.

## 7. Output

State the strongest verified claim, e.g.:

“weak duality only,” “zero gap but dual attainment not established,” or “strong duality with attainment under Assumptions A1–A3.”
